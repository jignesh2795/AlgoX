# AI-Era Ecosystem History

## Scope

AlgoX maintains a historical research program beginning in **January 2023** to study the evolution of open-source technology during the modern generative-AI era.

The program is not a simple GitHub trending archive. It combines historical popularity signals with technical, architectural, research, engineering, ecosystem, and outcome analysis.

## What to capture

For important projects and technologies:

- When they emerged
- Why they became important
- Trend/growth signals
- Problem they addressed
- Key capabilities
- Architecture and design patterns
- Dependencies and integrations
- Developer workflow innovations
- Research ideas introduced or popularized
- Limitations and failure modes
- Community and maintenance evolution
- Projects influenced by them
- Technologies that replaced or absorbed them
- Capabilities that remain useful today

## Classification

Each project may be classified as:

- **TRENDING** — receiving significant current attention
- **BREAKOUT** — unusually rapid growth or adoption
- **SUSTAINED** — continued importance over multiple periods
- **FALLEN** — once important but subsequently declining or abandoned

These are ecosystem signals, not quality judgments.

## Evaluation dimensions

Popularity is kept separate from technical quality. Relevant dimensions include growth, contributor activity, releases, architecture, code quality, tests, documentation, extensibility, reliability, ecosystem adoption, research value, production readiness, novelty, maintenance health, and license compatibility.

## Historical periods

- `2023/` — generative-AI and early agent explosion
- `2024/` — local models, RAG, multimodality, agent frameworks
- `2025/` — MCP, coding agents, memory, computer use, agentic development
- `2026/` — AI-native software engineering, agent infrastructure, persistent agents, routing, and emerging architectures

## Historical analysis questions

For each major shift ask:

1. What changed?
2. Why did it change?
3. What enabled the change?
4. Which projects demonstrated it first or most effectively?
5. What evidence supports its importance?
6. What failed or was overhyped?
7. What survived into later generations?
8. What capability should AlgoX retain?

## Source policy

Historical claims should be supported by dated sources, repository state/commit information where possible, release history, ecosystem evidence, and independent corroboration. A current repository state must not automatically be treated as evidence of its historical state.
