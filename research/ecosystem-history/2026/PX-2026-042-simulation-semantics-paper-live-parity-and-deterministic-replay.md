# PX-2026-042 — Simulation semantics, paper/live parity and deterministic replay

**Research date:** 2026-11-08  
**Status:** established engine documentation compared; AlgoX mode contract and acceptance requirements specified  
**AlgoX area:** backtest engine / paper and shadow execution / live adapter boundary / shared order lifecycle / deterministic replay / mode isolation  
**Inputs:** PX-025 R-001–R-255; PX-024–PX-040 order recovery, risk admission, market data, accounting, execution realism and statistical validation  
**Mode:** research and requirements specification only  
**Non-goals:** no trading-mode implementation, broker connection, market-data subscription, test order, live/paper order, account access, code changes, paid service, PR or merge to main

## Executive finding

A backtest, paper/dry-run and live trading system can share a strategy while still producing different fills and event timing. It is a category error to demand identical fills in all three modes:

- A **backtest** consumes historical observations and a simulation clock. Its order fills are generated by an explicit historical fill model.
- A **paper/dry-run** consumes current market observations but creates virtual orders/fills and virtual account state. It does not establish a real broker order or actual exchange execution.
- A **shadow** mode observes the live decision path and records hypothetical actions without creating an order at the broker. It is useful for comparing signals/admission decisions with actual market conditions but does not create actual fills.
- **Live** mode sends commands through the broker adapter and reconciles asynchronous broker/exchange/order/trade/account evidence. The broker and exchange determine actual acknowledgement and executions.

Freqtrade's documentation explicitly says its backtest assumes all orders fill, and that dry-run orders can remain unfilled (especially limits/no volume) and open a few seconds after the candle signal because of computation/dispatch latency. It recommends comparing backtest with dry-run and warns that results will differ. LEAN documents historical-model-driven fills in backtests versus asynchronous brokerage fills in live trading; live timing does not depend on subscription resolution. It also exposes fill/fee/slippage/margin models as extension points and notes that built-in models may assume complete fills unless a custom partial-fill model is used.

**Decision:** AlgoX should target **semantic parity, not fill parity**.

Semantic parity means that all modes use the same:
- strategy intent and canonical order request;
- canonical instrument/contract identity;
- market/session, position and portfolio definitions;
- independent PX-033 risk-admission gates;
- order/child-order lifecycle vocabulary and legal transitions;
- position, cash, fee and settlement accounting rules;
- event provenance and decision records.

Modes supply different execution, clock, account and external evidence adapters. Backtest fills, paper fills and live broker fills must be visibly typed as different origins. The system must measure and explain the drift between modes instead of hiding it behind a common “order succeeded” flag.

## 1. Comparison of established engine patterns

### 1.1 Freqtrade: backtest versus dry-run

Freqtrade's strategy quickstart distinguishes:
- **Backtesting:** historical data is downloaded from an exchange; the system assesses a strategy quickly but warns that results can be distorted and directs users to backtesting assumptions and look-ahead checks.
- **Dry-run/forward testing:** uses real-time exchange data and tracks signals/trades normally without opening trades on the exchange.

The same documentation says:
- backtests assume all orders fill;
- dry-run limit orders may not fill, including where exchange volume is absent;
- after a candle-close entry signal, backtest entry is assumed at the next candle open unless custom pricing is used;
- dry-run processing takes time after the new candle is received, so actual attempt timing can be seconds after candle open;
- those different entry prices create different ROI, stop-loss, trailing-stop and callback outcomes;
- users should compare backtest and dry-run and investigate signal/candle consistency and processing lag.

This is a direct example of why the engine must declare fill/timing assumptions and measure mode drift. AlgoX must not adopt the simplified “all orders fill” assumption as an unqualified default, especially given PX-040's price/quantity/depth and partial-fill requirements.

Official source:
- https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/strategy-101/

### 1.2 QuantConnect LEAN: shared order concepts, different fill sources

LEAN documents:
- backtest fills are simulated with historical data;
- fill, fee, slippage and margin models can be customised;
- live orders are sent to a brokerage API and fill asynchronously;
- live fill price and fee are supplied by the brokerage;
- live fill timing does not depend on the subscription resolution;
- backtest fill evaluation cadence depends on the data subscription/time-slice resolution;
- updates are not guaranteed because an order may fill before a modify request reaches the brokerage;
- the brokerage reports filled, partially filled and cancelled states;
- open orders are fetched from the brokerage on deployment to populate the transaction manager.

LEAN's Reality Modeling documentation says built-in backtest fill models commonly assume complete fills; a custom model is needed to simulate partial fills. It discusses stale fills and configurable stale-price thresholds. Its volume-share fill-model example limits a simulated fill to a configured fraction of bar volume and preserves remaining quantity.

This is an example of shared order abstractions with pluggable execution models. It is not proof that any one fill model or threshold is suitable for NSE.

Official sources:
- https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/live-trading/trading-and-orders
- https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/reality-modeling/trade-fills/key-concepts

### 1.3 Generalizable architecture lesson

The established systems show a useful separation:
- strategy decides intent;
- transaction/order manager owns the order lifecycle;
- mode/brokerage/fill model determines how an order is executed or simulated;
- order events update portfolio state;
- fees, slippage, margin and settlement are explicit models.

AlgoX should use the same separation, while strengthening provenance, fail-closed risk gates, Indian market settlement and cross-mode event replay required by PX-024–PX-041.

## 2. Define four operating modes precisely

### 2.1 BACKTEST

Inputs:
- pinned historical data and point-in-time universe;
- a replay/virtual clock;
- configured historical fill, fee, slippage, margin and settlement models;
- versioned strategy, risk policy, contract master, cost policy and experiment manifest.

Outputs:
- simulated admissions and orders;
- simulated fills/rejections/partial fills/cancellations;
- simulated positions, cash, fees, margin and settlement events;
- deterministic event log and replay manifest.

A backtest must never create a broker-side order. A simulated order/fill must use explicit event origin such as `SIMULATED_BACKTEST`; it is not an exchange execution.

### 2.2 PAPER / DRY-RUN

Inputs:
- current provider/exchange market feed with PX-037 freshness/completeness rules;
- current contract and provider master;
- virtual account balance/positions/fees/margin model;
- same strategy intent and independent admission gates as live, with a paper execution adapter.

Outputs:
- virtual order state and fills generated by the paper fill model;
- virtual position/cash/fee/margin ledger;
- current-market latency and fill-quality measurements;
- complete mode/source provenance.

A paper order must not call the live broker place/modify/cancel endpoint. The UI, CLI and API must show that its fills are simulated. A current quote used in paper mode does not turn a virtual fill into an actual execution.

### 2.3 SHADOW

Inputs:
- live market data and current account/portfolio observations where authorized for read-only observation;
- the same strategy/risk decision pipeline and live-like timing;
- no broker command capability in the shadow runtime.

Outputs:
- signals, intended orders, admission results and projected exposure;
- hypothetical fill outcomes from a separately declared shadow fill model, if enabled;
- comparison against later market/broker observations where legitimately available.

Shadow mode must be unable to submit orders. A hypothetical fill must remain distinct from an actual external trade. If a real account's orders are observed for comparison, they must remain external observations and never be attributed to AlgoX's hypothetical order.

### 2.4 LIVE

Inputs:
- live feed and reference data with current integrity/freshness;
- actual broker/account credentials within a separate live execution boundary;
- verified broker account/client/entity identities, broker-specific order contract and live risk data.

Outputs:
- actual dispatch attempts and transport results;
- broker acknowledgements and order IDs;
- exchange order IDs/status where supplied;
- actual trade/fill events from broker/exchange evidence;
- reconciled actual positions, cash, holdings, charges, margin and settlement evidence.

Live state must be based on actual external evidence rather than the expected result of a simulation. Unknown transport/broker outcomes remain UNKNOWN and must be recovered under PX-024–PX-027.

## 3. Semantic parity versus execution parity

### 3.1 What must be shared

All modes should reuse the same domain definitions and pure decision logic for:
- canonical intent and order request construction;
- instrument/contract identity and quantity units;
- market session and exchange calendar;
- order state and legal transitions;
- independent risk gates and projected-exposure calculation;
- lot/tick/freeze, position-limit and settlement-mode validation;
- portfolio position and cash-ledger roll-forward;
- fee component taxonomy and policy effective dates;
- event provenance, decision reasons and audit identifiers.

The same risk policy must be invoked in every mode. If a data field is not available in backtest or paper, its state is UNKNOWN/not simulated under an explicit mode policy; the risk gate must not be silently skipped merely because the mode is not live.

### 3.2 What must be mode-specific

The following are intentionally mode-specific adapters or models:

| Concern | Backtest | Paper / dry-run | Shadow | Live |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clock | Historical virtual clock | Live wall/monotonic clock | Live wall/monotonic clock | Live wall/monotonic clock |
| Market observations | Pinned historical data | Current provider feed | Current provider feed | Current provider feed |
| Order destination | Simulation adapter | Virtual-only adapter | No command adapter | Broker adapter |
| Fill evidence | Historical fill model | Paper fill model | Hypothetical fill model or none | Broker/exchange trade evidence |
| Account | Synthetic account | Virtual account | Projected/observed reference account | Actual broker account |
| Fees | Historical dated cost policy | Explicit paper tariff model | Hypothetical cost model | Actual broker contract note/ledger plus dated rules |
| Margin | Simulated/dated risk model | Virtual margin/collateral model | Projected margin check | Actual broker/clearing/account evidence |
| Latency | Replay/recorded timing | Measured local/provider timing | Measured decision timing | Actual dispatch/ack/fill timing |
| Settlement | Simulated settlement model | Virtual settlement model | Hypothetical obligation | Actual settlement and ledger evidence |

A mode must identify its adapter/model versions in every run manifest. A paper fill model must not be passed off as a broker fill model merely because both use current prices.

### 3.3 The parity contract

A parity comparison should evaluate:
- signal identity and timestamp (subject to declared source/data timing);
- canonical instrument/contract selected;
- normalized order intent and parameters;
- risk gate outcomes and gate input versions;
- order state transitions;
- quantity/partial-fill and residual outcomes;
- position and cash ledger after each event;
- fees/margin/settlement components;
- reason codes for deviations.

It must **not** require the fill price or filled quantity to match across modes where the fill sources differ. Instead, differences are categorised and measured:
- signal drift;
- data-vintage/timestamp drift;
- risk-input/admission drift;
- request/adapter mapping drift;
- simulated-vs-actual fill drift;
- timing/latency drift;
- fee/margin/settlement drift;
- unresolved external outcome.

## 4. Shared order lifecycle and event model

A mode-neutral domain lifecycle can be modelled as:

`IntentCreated` → `AdmissionEvaluated` → `AdmissionAllowed` / `AdmissionDenied` / `AdmissionUnknown` → `SubmissionAttempted` → mode-specific submission evidence → `Accepted/Working` → `PartiallyFilled` → `Filled` / `Cancelled` / `Rejected` / `Expired` / `Unknown`.

Mode-specific interpretation:
- Backtest: `SimulatedSubmissionAccepted`, `SimulatedFillGenerated`, `SimulatedReject`.
- Paper: `PaperSubmissionAccepted`, `PaperFillGenerated`, `PaperReject`.
- Shadow: `ShadowIntentRecorded`, optionally `ShadowFillEstimated`; no submission event.
- Live: `BrokerDispatchAttempted`, `BrokerAcknowledged`, `ExchangeAcknowledged`, `BrokerTradeObserved`, `ReconciliationObserved`.

These event types may share domain transition semantics but must not be semantically interchangeable. A `SimulatedFillGenerated` must never be consumed by a live ledger as if it were a broker trade.

The canonical portfolio/accounting processor can consume typed economic execution events while preserving the source origin and mode. It must not merge unrelated virtual and live accounts into a shared balance.

## 5. Event clocks and scheduling

### 5.1 Replay clock

Backtest should use an injected virtual clock advanced by the event scheduler. Every event records:
- market/event time;
- simulated receive/arrival time if modelled;
- processing sequence/time;
- source observation and data-manifest identity;
- scheduled callback/timer identity.

Timers, bar close callbacks, order-arrival delay, stop triggers, expiry and settlement must run against the virtual clock. They must not depend on actual host wall-clock sleeps.

### 5.2 Paper/shadow/live clocks

Paper, shadow and live run against real clocks. Capture UTC wall time and monotonic elapsed time, source event timestamps and processing latency as separate domains (PX-037). Broker events may arrive asynchronously between market bars.

The same strategy should receive a stable event ordering contract, but the engine must not force asynchronous live broker callbacks into an artificial “after candle close only” schedule. If an order fill arrives between bars, update the domain portfolio/order state promptly and ensure the next strategy decision uses the latest reconciled state according to a declared consistency boundary.

### 5.3 Deterministic replay of live/shadow events

Where live/shadow observations are persisted, replay should use the recorded raw event order and recorded arrival/processing sequence, not sorted exchange timestamp alone. If the provider does not supply a sequence number, the recorded local arrival order is replayable but cannot be claimed as exchange causal order. Missing/gap intervals remain UNKNOWN under PX-027/PX-037.

Replay with the same inputs, policy/configuration, seed and scheduler version should produce the same **domain decisions and simulated outputs**. It cannot recreate the broker's actual external execution by rerunning a deterministic simulation; the external broker event log is the evidence of what actually happened.

## 6. Mode isolation and safety controls

### 6.1 No paper-to-live route bleed

Mode must be explicit in:
- process/run configuration;
- strategy intent and admission snapshot;
- order adapter instance;
- account/portfolio namespace;
- audit/event log;
- persisted record and UI display.

Paper and shadow components must not possess or resolve a live order-submission capability. A paper mode flag in a user interface is not enough if the backend can still call the live adapter.

### 6.2 Mode transition requires an explicit boundary

Switching BACKTEST/PAPER/SHADOW/LIVE must:
1. stop new order intents;
2. drain or explicitly preserve pending commands/events;
3. reconcile the current mode's virtual/actual account state;
4. bind the new mode to the correct account and adapter;
5. perform the new mode's startup checks;
6. create a new mode epoch and immutable run manifest;
7. require the appropriate explicit live authorization for LIVE.

A pending simulated or paper order must never be rehydrated as a live broker order. A live open order must not be cloned into a paper account. Virtual balances/positions must never be silently copied into an actual live account.

### 6.3 Credentials and capabilities

The live broker adapter must be a separately injected capability; no credential or connection object should be present in backtest/paper/shadow runtime. The mode resolver must fail closed if a configuration requests LIVE but the approved live capability, account binding and authorisation context are absent or ambiguous. Conversely, a PAPER configuration must not fall back to live because a paper adapter failed.

## 7. Parity and drift reporting

Every comparative run should have a common strategy/version and a set of linked run IDs. The parity report should compare:
- signal counts and signal timestamps;
- instrument identity and order-intent fingerprints;
- gate-by-gate admission outcomes/reasons;
- order request parameters;
- order and child-order state transitions;
- fills, price/quantity, unfilled residual and order lifetime;
- position/portfolio/cash-ledger snapshots;
- fees, margin, settlement and risk metrics;
- processing/dispatch/fill latency.

Differences should be attributed, not erased:
- backtest fill vs paper simulated fill;
- paper fill vs actual live trade;
- data source/vintage change;
- provider feed delay or disconnect;
- order dispatch latency;
- rejected/modified/cancelled order;
- quantity or price rounding/contract-master drift;
- brokerage/fee/tax difference;
- actual settlement or corporate action.

A parity threshold may trigger investigation, but no universal numeric threshold is claimed by this research. It must be set by product, frequency, strategy, order type and use case. A parity report is not proof of live profitability.

## 8. Additional requirements for PX-025

### R-256 — Operating mode is explicit and typed

Every strategy run, intent, order, account, fill and ledger event MUST carry an explicit BACKTEST, PAPER, SHADOW or LIVE mode identity and run/mode epoch. A missing or conflicting mode MUST fail closed.

### R-257 — Strategy and domain risk semantics are shared across modes

All modes MUST invoke the same canonical strategy-intent contract, instrument identity, projected-exposure calculation, independent risk-admission gates and order/portfolio state-transition rules. Mode-specific adapters may supply different evidence/execution results but MUST NOT silently omit a mandatory domain risk gate.

### R-258 — Fill and submission origins are mode-specific

Backtest, paper and shadow-generated order/fill events MUST have explicit simulated origin types. They MUST NOT be represented as broker acknowledgements, exchange orders or actual trades. Live fills MUST be sourced from broker/exchange evidence and reconciled under PX-024–PX-028.

### R-259 — Backtest fill assumptions are explicit and versioned

Every backtest MUST pin its fill model, data resolution, order-arrival timing, price/quantity availability, partial-fill policy, stale-price threshold, session restrictions, slippage and fee models. An “all orders fill” assumption MUST NOT be the implicit default or be presented as realistic without explicit qualification.

### R-260 — Paper and shadow modes cannot submit live orders

Paper and shadow runtimes MUST NOT possess a callable live order-placement, modification or cancellation capability. A mode label or UI toggle alone is insufficient; backend capability routing MUST enforce the restriction. Adapter failure MUST NOT fall back from paper to live.

### R-261 — Shadow mode does not create exchange execution claims

Shadow mode MUST record hypothetical intents/admission outcomes and, if configured, estimated fills as hypothetical. It MUST NOT create a broker dispatch, exchange acknowledgement, actual fill or live portfolio mutation. Observed external account events MUST remain external observations.

### R-262 — Live order lifecycle is asynchronous

Live broker dispatch, acknowledgement, partial/full fill, rejection, cancellation and modification MUST be processed as asynchronous external observations. Strategy code MUST NOT assume that a submit call implies acceptance or that a modify/cancel request takes effect before a broker event confirms it.

### R-263 — One shared economic event model preserves mode provenance

The portfolio/accounting layer MAY consume normalized execution/settlement event contracts across modes, but MUST retain the original event type, mode, adapter/fill-model origin and source references. Simulated, paper and live events MUST remain distinguishable and MUST NOT contaminate one another's account namespace.

### R-264 — Replay uses a virtual clock and deterministic scheduler

Backtests MUST use an injected virtual clock and deterministic event scheduler for data, timers, signals, order arrivals, fills, expiry and settlement. The same pinned inputs/configuration/seed/scheduler version MUST reproduce the same simulated event sequence and domain decisions.

### R-265 — Live event replay preserves observed arrival order and uncertainty

A replay of captured paper/shadow/live events MUST preserve recorded receive/processing sequence and connection epoch. It MUST NOT sort solely by provider event time and claim exchange causal ordering when no provider sequence contract exists. Gap intervals MUST remain UNKNOWN as specified in PX-027/PX-037.

### R-266 — Mode transitions require an isolated state boundary

A mode transition MUST stop new intents, resolve/preserve pending activity, create a new mode epoch, bind the correct account/adapter, run startup reconciliation and record a new manifest. Simulated/paper orders and balances MUST NOT be rehydrated into live; live open orders MUST NOT be silently cloned into virtual accounts.

### R-267 — Live credentials and adapter capabilities are isolated

Broker credentials, sessions and live order capabilities MUST be injected only into an explicitly authorized LIVE runtime. BACKTEST/PAPER/SHADOW processes MUST NOT receive live credentials or a callable live adapter. Missing or ambiguous live account/capability MUST block LIVE.

### R-268 — Paper account balances and fills are virtual and labelled

Paper account funds, positions, collateral, fees and fills MUST be separately namespaced, explicitly virtual and derived from the paper model. A paper balance or fill MUST NOT be shown as actual broker account evidence or actual client performance.

### R-269 — Market-data and order-processing latency are measured separately

Each mode MUST preserve market event time, local receive time, strategy decision time, dispatch time, broker acknowledgement time and fill time where available. Backtest latency is a declared simulation assumption; paper/shadow/live latency is measured. The same timestamp MUST NOT be reused for different stages.

### R-270 — Shared risk gates use mode-appropriate evidence without bypass

All mandatory PX-033 risk gates MUST run in every mode. If a gate's live evidence is unavailable in backtest/paper, the result MUST be simulated from pinned effective data or be UNKNOWN/not simulated under an explicit policy. The engine MUST NOT turn an unavailable gate into PASS or skip it silently.

### R-271 — Backtest/paper/live drift is measured and attributable

A parity report MUST compare signals, canonical instruments, admission results, request intents, lifecycle transitions, fill quantities/prices, residuals, positions, cash, costs and settlement. Differences MUST be attributed to data, timing, model, provider, broker or market causes where evidence allows. It MUST NOT require identical fills across modes.

### R-272 — Mode-specific fill/fee/margin/settlement models are pinned

Each run MUST preserve the exact fill, fee, slippage, margin, funding, risk and settlement model version used for that mode. A paper or live model MUST NOT silently inherit a backtest assumption, and a broker-reported fee/fill MUST not be overwritten by a simulated model.

### R-273 — Partial fills and residual orders behave consistently in domain accounting

All modes MUST use the same order residual, cumulative filled quantity, average fill price and position/ledger roll-forward invariants. The source of each fill may differ, but the engine MUST NOT assume complete fills in one mode while allowing partial fills in another without an explicitly versioned model difference.

### R-274 — Event-driven callbacks and bar callbacks have explicit ordering

The scheduler MUST define how market-data events, broker/order events, timers, bar closes and strategy callbacks are sequenced. Live fills arriving between bars MUST update domain state at the recorded event boundary; a later bar callback MUST not use stale pre-fill portfolio state.

### R-275 — Comparative run manifest is immutable and mode-scoped

Every parity/comparison run MUST pin strategy hash/version, mode, run epoch, canonical data manifest, contract crosswalk, risk policy, fill/fee/margin/settlement model, clock/scheduler, random seed, account namespace and source event logs. The comparison MUST identify which dimensions are directly comparable and which are intentionally mode-specific.

## 10. Open questions and implementation boundaries

1. Which backtest fill model fidelity is achievable with the available NSE historical bar/tick/depth data for each strategy horizon?
2. Which paper fill model should be used for each product/order type: quote touch, trade-through, depth consumption, queue estimate or conservative hybrid?
3. How will actual live broker order events be recorded between bar callbacks without introducing data races or nondeterministic strategy state?
4. Which parts of the simulator should be deterministic event-replay code and which should be randomised fill models with seeded repeatability?
5. What freshness/latency thresholds are appropriate by instrument, frequency and order type? PX-037 requires explicit per-field policies but does not set one universal threshold.
6. How should local broker margin estimates be used in paper mode where client collection/collateral evidence is not available? The result may need to remain simulated/UNKNOWN rather than treated as actual.
7. What exact account migration/reconciliation policy applies if a user deliberately transitions from paper to live? No automatic transfer of paper positions/orders is allowed.
8. How will parity metrics be calibrated once real paper and live evidence exists? No historical observations were measured in this phase.

These questions are deferred to a design and controlled validation phase. None authorizes live operation.

## 11. Research decisions and non-authorization

- **ACCEPT:** one canonical strategy/risk/order/portfolio semantic core shared across modes.
- **ACCEPT:** distinct, explicitly typed backtest, paper, shadow and live execution adapters.
- **ACCEPT:** shared risk gates and lifecycle semantics; mode-specific evidence/fill models.
- **ACCEPT:** virtual clock and deterministic scheduler for historical replay; actual wall/monotonic timing for paper/shadow/live.
- **ACCEPT:** explicit paper/live capability isolation and non-transferable virtual balances/orders.
- **ACCEPT:** separate simulated fills, paper fills, shadow estimates and broker/exchange execution evidence.
- **ACCEPT:** parity is measured and deviations attributed; identical fills are not required or promised.
- **DEFER:** exact fill-model choice, scheduler concurrency design, paper quote/depth policy, live adapter integration and numerical parity thresholds until controlled sample evidence is available.
- **NOT AUTHORIZED:** engine implementation, broker connection, market-feed subscription, paper/live order, production operation, paid service, code changes, PR or merge to main.

**Final status:** PX-2026-042 simulation semantics, paper/live parity and deterministic replay research documented. PX-025 expanded to 275 requirements and 490 proposed acceptance scenarios. No engine, broker, paper/live account, strategy or test was run.
