# AlgoX Research Methodology

## 1. Research question first

Every meaningful investigation starts with a research question (`RQ-*`). The question defines scope, target decisions, evidence requirements, and expected outputs.

## 2. Multi-source discovery

Research should triangulate across implementation, research, operational, market, regulatory, and community sources. GitHub is an important implementation source, not the complete knowledge base.

## 3. Evidence separation

Keep these distinct:

- **Observation** — directly supported by a source.
- **Finding** — AlgoX interpretation of evidence.
- **Hypothesis** — proposition requiring testing.
- **Decision** — an explicit conclusion based on evidence and/or experiments.

## 4. Source-specific reliability

Reliability depends on the claim. Official exchange documentation is authoritative for exchange rules; source code and tests are stronger for implementation behavior; empirical papers and reproductions matter for algorithmic claims; production evidence matters for operational claims.

## 5. Extraction

Extract reusable knowledge at four levels:

- Code reuse
- Algorithm reuse
- Architecture/design inspiration
- General knowledge

Record provenance and licensing for anything that may be reused.

## 6. Validation

Promising ideas move through:

`UNVERIFIED → REPRODUCED → BENCHMARKED → VALIDATED`

A reproduction must record environment, version/commit, dataset, configuration, parameters, and relevant results.

## 7. Decision

Use one of five decisions: ADOPT, ADAPT, WRAP, BUILD, REJECT. Decisions should reference requirements, alternatives, evidence, experiments, trade-offs, and confidence.

## 8. Institutional memory

Do not delete failed ideas from the knowledge base merely because they were rejected. Record why they failed so future projects do not repeat the investigation.

## 9. Historical ecosystem research

For technology-history research, preserve the time dimension. Record the project state, trend evidence, major releases, architectural shifts, adoption signals, and eventual outcome. The AI-era program begins in January 2023.
